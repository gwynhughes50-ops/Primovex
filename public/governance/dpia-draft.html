What has changed since version 0.1

Version 0.1 covered Concerns, SARs, Inventory and Temperature. Primovex has since gained four things that process personal data in new ways, and a fifth, Significant events, added on 8 October 2026. Each is described below and has its own risks and measures.

1. Staff accounts, departments and the organisation chart (Step 2B).

2. Sign-in and activity records: a report of when staff signed in, which areas of the app they used and for how long (Step 2C). This is a form of monitoring of staff.

3. The Orb's AI language assistant, which uses Microsoft Azure OpenAI (Step 2D). Two separate switches, both off by default.

4. Staff-to-staff messages and reminders sent through the app (Step 2E).

Technical safeguards have also grown (Appendix A). Several decisions are needed from the DPO before sign-off (Appendix C).

STEP 1

Identify the need for a DPIA

Primovex is a practice management system used for stock and medicines inventory, cold-chain temperature monitoring, compliance recording, staff administration and governance case management, specifically Patient Safety Concerns (following the NHS Wales "Listening to People" pathway) and Subject Access Requests. It is introduced alongside the practice's core national systems (EMIS, Docman), not as a replacement for them.

At least one ICO screening trigger applies, which is enough on its own to require a DPIA. Four now apply:

  • Special category data. Concerns, SARs and Significant events relate to patient care and, even pseudonymised, fall within Article 9 health data by association.
  • New technology. Governance case management in a dedicated tool, and now the use of generative AI (Azure OpenAI) inside the app, have not previously been assessed.
  • Systematic monitoring of employees. The sign-in and activity records (Step 2C) record, for every member of staff, when they used the system and which areas they used.
  • Systematic, practice-wide processing, used continuously by multiple staff roles.

Conclusion: a DPIA is required before Primovex's governance modules are relied upon as the practice's primary record, and before the AI features or the staff activity report are used with real staff.

STEP 2

Describe the processing

A. Governance and operations (unchanged from version 0.1)

ItemDetail
CollectionManually entered by practice staff on desktop or mobile at the point a concern, SAR, significant event, stock movement or temperature reading occurs.
Significant events (added 8 October 2026)Any member of staff can report a significant event. It records what happened (in words that name no one), the date, room, kind of event, level of harm and, if a patient was involved, their EMIS number (or initials and date of birth if there is none), never a name. Staff are referred to by role; the team can link staff by account to follow or review an event. Investigation findings, each reviewer's own review, the notes of significant event meetings (who attended, what was said about each event) and agreed actions are stored. People see only events they reported, lead, are named on or were asked to review; a Significant events team and partners (oversight) see all. Reviews and notes are kept for the life of the record like Concerns; the timeline cannot be edited or deleted. Notifications carry the reference and title only.
UseCase tracking against statutory and pathway deadlines, correspondence logging, stock and cold-chain safety monitoring, staff-facing reporting (including annual category and outcome breakdowns for external returns such as the Welsh Risk Pool).
StorageGoogle Cloud Firestore, EU multi-region (eur3). Application server logic (Cloud Functions) runs in europe-west2 (London, UK).
DeletionGovernance timeline, correspondence and stock-movement entries are immutable audit records. Stock items are archived (recoverable); permanently deleting a stock item needs a specific permission that only a System Admin or a role granted it holds. Concern cases and user accounts can be hard-deleted, restricted to System Admin, behind a confirmation step.
SharingConcerns correspondence is logged with named external bodies as part of the normal governance process. See the data-sharing map for the full list and current protocol status.
Special category dataYes, by association: patient identifiers (EMIS number, or initials plus date of birth) and, for the person raising a concern when that differs from the patient, initials and a contact method. B. Staff accounts, departments and the organisation chart (new)

ItemDetail
DataFor each member of staff: name, work email address, job role, department, who they report to, and whether they are highlighted on the chart. Their sign-in password is held by Google (Firebase Authentication), never by Primovex.
SourceEntered by a System Admin, singly or by importing a spreadsheet of the practice's staff list (approximately 60 people). The import shows exactly what it will do before it does anything and does not send any email; each person is later given a sign-in link by the administrator.
UseGiving each person the access their job needs (role-based permissions), assigning tasks and SARs, showing the organisation chart, and printing the chart or a staff list by department.
Who can see itThe staff list and chart are visible only to System Admins and roles granted user-management permission. Other staff see only a name and role where they must choose a colleague (for example to assign a SAR).
Desktop orb and tray (Windows)On the Windows desktop app, closing the window can hide Primovex to the system tray instead of quitting, and a small always-on-top Pulse orb shows on the desktop while the window is minimised or hidden. The orb shows only a score and a one-line status such as a count of items. While Primovex runs in the tray the person stays signed in; the 15-minute lock and 12-hour session limit still apply. Each person can switch both off in the Pulse settings, and signing out quits as normal. Shared computers should be locked or signed out at the end of a shift under the practice's usual policy.
Paper copiesThe organisation chart and staff list can be printed. Printed copies are outside the system's controls and should be handled under the practice's normal paper-record policy.

C. Sign-in and activity records (new): monitoring of staff

Primovex records when people sign in and which parts of the app they use, so that an administrator can review access for security and audit purposes.

ItemDetail
What is recordedName and role (taken from the person's own account, not from what the app says), when a session started and ended or was last active, the type of device (for example Windows or Android) and a random device identifier created by the app, how the session ended (signed out, timed out, closed), and, per area of the app, how many times it was visited and how long the person was actively there. An "area" is only the top one or two levels of the address, for example Inventory or Governance / SARs.
What is not recordedAnything typed or searched, which record was opened, any patient or concern detail, the content of any screen. Time spent is only counted while the window is visible and the person has used the screen or keyboard within the last five minutes.
How it is protectedThe times and the person's identity are set by the server, not the device, and the server will not credit more active time than has actually passed, so a person cannot alter their own record. Nobody can edit or delete the records from the app; only the server writes them.
Who can see itSystem Admins, and any role that has been given the audit-read permission. Built-in roles with audit-read: System Admin and Practice Manager. The practice can create or change roles to narrow this.
Flags shown to the reviewerSign-ins outside 06:00 to 22:00, sessions over 12 hours, and being signed in on two devices at the same time. These are prompts to look, not findings.
Related audit entriesThe governed audit ledger records password sign-ins and sign-outs, and an entry when the app is opened with an existing sign-in. (Recorded without any app content.)
RetentionSign-in and activity records are deleted automatically after 12 months by a weekly clean-up. The audit ledger is append-only and follows its own retention schedule. The 12-month period is a proposal for the DPO to approve.
Not capturedFailed sign-in attempts happen before anyone is identified and are held by Firebase Authentication, not Primovex.
Purpose limit (proposed)Security, audit and the investigation of suspected misuse. Not to be used for routine performance management. The controller must decide and record this.

D. The Orb's AI language assistant (new)

The Orb is Primovex's assistant. It answers questions about the practice (stock, fridges, cleaning, alerts and similar) from approved lookups, and can now also prepare two actions for a person to confirm: a short message to a team (everyone with a role such as HCA) and a reorder request for a stock item. Nothing is sent or created until the person presses Confirm on a card that shows exactly what will happen; the card expires after 15 minutes, can be used once, and is checked against the person's permissions again. The messages can't contain numbers, dates, email addresses or phone numbers, so patient details are refused, and each use is recorded in the audit trail. Until recently it understood only fixed phrases. Two optional AI features now use Microsoft Azure OpenAI. Both are switched off until a System Admin turns each one on, with a confirmation that the region and this DPIA have been checked. They are independent.

Switch 1: understanding questionsSwitch 2: wording the answers

If it contains any number not in the facts, drops a warning (out of range, expired, out of stock), or contains a link, it is discarded and the original wording is used.

  • Processor: Microsoft (Azure OpenAI). The Azure resource is the one already used by ClinFlow. Its region is UK South (stated by the Practice Manager on 7 October 2026). Microsoft's retention of prompts for abuse monitoring and the data processing terms are To be completed (see Appendix C). The calls are made from the practice's Firebase server in London; the key is never in the app.
  • No decision about a person is made by the AI. It only chooses a lookup or rewords an answer, and the AI never carries anything out. All lookups only read. The only changes the Orb can make are the two above (a team message, a reorder request), and only after the person presses Confirm.
  • Free-text risk: staff could type a patient's name into the Orb. Numbers and dates are removed automatically, but names cannot be reliably detected. Staff guidance is required (Step 6).

E. Staff messages, reminders and notifications (new)

Primovex sends in-app notifications: SAR and concern deadline reminders to the assigned person and, when overdue, their manager; a message from one colleague to another about a kit item during a check (up to 300 characters, typed by the sender); and a reminder to oneself. Messages are visible only to the recipient. Free text typed into a message is a place where someone could put identifying detail; staff guidance covers this. How long notifications are kept in an inbox is To be completed and needs a retention decision.

Context

Patients do not interact with Primovex and have no visibility of it as a distinct system. Patients raising a Concern shortly after a clinical incident may be vulnerable at the point data is recorded, which is a reason for the deliberate identifier minimisation already built in. Staff are the people most affected by the new processing: they will be recorded using the system and may use the AI assistant. Their reasonable expectations, and what they have been told, matter (Step 4).

Purposes

To record and manage patient safety concerns and SARs within their timescales with an auditable trail; to support cold-chain safety and accurate stock; to give staff the right access and help them find information quickly; and to keep a reviewable record of system access for security and audit. The intended effect on individuals is faster, more consistent handling of their concern or request, and a lower risk of a cold-chain failure.

STEP 3

Consultation process

To be completed by the practice. This draft was produced from technical review, not consultation. Before sign-off, consider recording:

  • Consultation with the staff who will use Concerns/SARs day to day on whether the recorded fields and workflow match how they work.
  • Consultation with staff (and any representative body) about the sign-in and activity records and the Orb's AI features, before they are relied upon. Monitoring staff without telling them, or without a clear purpose, is a significant risk.
  • Consultation with BCUHB's Information Governance team on the data-sharing implications in the data-sharing map.
  • Whether patient or PPG input is appropriate for a system patients do not interact with: a judgement for the DPO.
