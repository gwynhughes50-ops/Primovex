Data-sharing map · for WASPI / ISP review

Who Primovex data currently flows to

Every external organisation Primovex's Concerns module logs correspondence with, compiled from the categories built into the system. This is not itself a Wales Accord on the Sharing of Personal Information (WASPI) Information Sharing Protocol (ISP) — it's the input list for checking whether an existing BCUHB/health-board ISP already covers each flow, or whether a new one or a Data Disclosure Agreement is needed.

Why this matters now

Primovex has always let staff log that a concern was shared with, say, MDDUS or the police — but until this review, nothing had checked whether that sharing sits under an agreed protocol. That's exactly the gap WASPI exists to close. None of these flows are new because of Primovex; Primovex just makes them visible and auditable for the first time.

Updated 4 November 2026

This map was first written on 18 September 2026 for the Concerns module only. It now also records what has been added since: how ClinFlow handles clinical letters, the Microsoft services behind it, the shared folder, and the Google Cloud services the whole system runs on. Three things to note. (1) What is said above about never sharing a patient's name is true of Concerns and SARs. It would not be true of ClinFlow if real letters were ever allowed, because a hospital letter names the patient. ClinFlow is under development and is used with invented test letters only, and that does not change until it has been assessed in the DPIA. (2) The statement below that technical third parties receive no patient data was true when written and is corrected: the Microsoft services used by ClinFlow would receive patient letters. (3) Everything marked "to confirm" still needs the practice's own WASPI, contract and data-processing records, which this review cannot see.

External bodies

What's shared is consistently limited to: case reference, category, an anonymised summary, and correspondence dates/notes — never the patient's name, and never more identifying detail than the pseudonymised identifier already described in the DPIA.

Concerns: dealings with outside parties (added October 2026)

Protocol status: to confirm

Primovex now records each step with an outside party on a concern, so the case shows where it is up to. The parties are the practice's indemnity insurer, GMPI, the Health Board and the solicitor, and anyone else the team types in by name (for example an expert, a regulator or another practice). For each step Primovex records the date, what happened (sent to them, they want changes, they need more information, they accepted it, GMPI instructed a solicitor), what kind of thing was sent (the draft final response, a reply to what they asked for, records or documents, or something else) and a short note. The documents themselves are sent outside Primovex, by post or email, as before. A step recorded by mistake is never deleted: it is struck through with the reason. These parties are the same bodies listed above, so the same protocol questions apply.

Data shared: none by Primovex; Primovex holds the record that something was sent, when, to whom, and a short noteDirection: practice → party, and replies backPrinted: this record appears on the printed case summary for anyone who prints it

Hospitals and other organisations that send letters to the practice (ClinFlow)

Not a sharing-out flow

ClinFlow helps the coding team deal with incoming clinical letters from hospitals, clinics and other services. These arrive as scans and are filed in Docman as they always have been. ClinFlow reads them and suggests a summary and codes; it sends nothing back to the organisation that wrote the letter, and it does not write to EMIS or Docman. The only recipients of the letter's content are the Microsoft services listed under technical third parties below. Used with invented test letters only at present.

Data shared with the sender: noneDirection: organisation → practice (incoming only)Protocol: receiving letters is part of normal care and needs no protocol of its own; ClinFlow is under development and is not yet covered by the DPIA

Technical third parties

Distinct from the bodies above: these receive data automatically as a side effect of a feature working, not because a member of staff chose to share a case. Most receive no patient data and are not a WASPI matter in themselves. The exception is ClinFlow, whose Microsoft services would receive patient letters if real letters were ever allowed; that is not permitted until ClinFlow has been assessed in the DPIA. Processors such as Microsoft and Google need a contract and a data processing agreement rather than a WASPI protocol. All of them belong on the record for the DPIA's third-party section.

Microsoft Azure OpenAI (the Orb, and ClinFlow)

Agreement and region: to confirm

Used in two places. For the Orb's optional AI language assistant, Primovex sends a typed question with numbers, dates, email addresses and phone numbers removed, and for the wording feature the facts of an answer (item, room, fridge names, counts). For ClinFlow, the text of a clinical letter goes here after it has been read, and a structured summary comes back. The calls are made from Primovex's server in London, never from the app, and the key is never in the app. The resource is stated to be in UK South (7 October 2026). The AI makes suggestions or rewords text; it decides nothing.

Data shared: Orb: scrubbed questions and answer facts. ClinFlow: letter text, invented test letters only today; a real letter would hold a patient's name, date of birth, NHS number and clinical detailNever shared: any stock, staff, patient or governance data through the Orb; no case or SAR dataTo confirm: Microsoft's data processing terms, retention of prompts for abuse monitoring, no training on the data, and the region (DPIA Appendix C item 5, for the Orb; ClinFlow is not yet covered by the DPIA)

Microsoft Azure Document Intelligence (ClinFlow)

Agreement and region: to confirm

Reads the text out of a scanned clinical letter. ClinFlow sends the letter (a PDF of up to 4 MB) from a Primovex server in London; the text comes back. The server keeps only a record that a reading took place (file name, a fingerprint of the file, size, page and word counts), never the letter or its text.

Data shared: the letter PDF; invented test letters only todayRegion: not yet confirmed (the OpenAI resource above is stated as UK South)To confirm: data processing terms, retention of what is sent, region (to be assessed in the DPIA before ClinFlow is used with real letters)

Microsoft OneDrive (the ClinFlow shared folder, in testing)

Tenant and sharing: to confirm

To let two computers see the same letters, ClinFlow can keep them in a shared folder. For testing this is a folder in a Microsoft OneDrive that the two computers sync (the work PC shows "OneDrive - NHS Wales"; which account and tenant it is has not been confirmed). The intended home is a practice network folder visible only to named people (stated by the Practice Manager in late October 2026). Each letter is three files: its reading and decisions, the scan, and later a short note with no patient details. A finished letter's files are deleted, but OneDrive keeps deleted files in its recycle bin for a period.

Data shared: the letter's reading, decisions and notes, and the scan; invented test letters only today; reference lists (no personal data)Who can see it: whoever the folder is shared with, which Primovex cannot checkTo confirm: tenant, who it is shared with, recycle-bin retention (to be assessed in the DPIA before ClinFlow is used with real letters)

Google Cloud / Firebase (the platform Primovex runs on)

Data processing addendum: to confirm it is on file

Primovex's database, sign-in, file storage, server code, website hosting and phone alerts are Google Firebase services. The database and file storage are in the EU (eur3) and the server code runs in London (europe-west2). On an iPhone, a spoken question is turned into words by Google Cloud Speech-to-Text through its EU endpoint: the recording (up to 20 seconds) is held in memory for that one request and is not stored. Alerts on a phone's lock screen are carried by Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple's or Google's push service; a lock screen shows only a short line and, for governance matters, only "You have a new notification". This is the platform every flow in this map runs on and was not listed here before.

Data shared: everything Primovex stores: staff accounts, concern, SAR and event records, stock, timesheets, and so on, as described in the DPIAHosting: EU for data; UK for server codeTo confirm: the data processing addendum is on file; the UK-only question (DPIA Appendix C items 8 and 5)

Firebase App Distribution (Android test builds)

Low: tester addresses only

New Android builds are sent to named testers through Firebase App Distribution, by email link. The only personal data involved is the testers' email addresses (two at present). No case, patient or staff records are in a build.

Data shared: two tester email addressesNever shared: any patient, staff, case or SAR data

Docman and EMIS (the practice's own systems)

No data flows from Primovex

Primovex has no connection to Docman or EMIS. ClinFlow saves a one-page summary PDF (and the original letter) into two folders on the practice's own network, and the practice's usual Docman process takes it from there. Nothing is sent to EMIS. If a supplier ever asks the practice to create a user account on EMIS so that software can sign in as a person, that is a new data flow and needs the DPO's and Caldicott Guardian's agreement first (DPIA Appendix C item 18). A future ClinFlow link to EMIS should go through EMIS's approved partner route and would need this map and the DPIA to be updated.

Data shared: noneDirection: none

QR code image service (api.qrserver.com)

Resolved — no longer used

Until 18 September 2026, printing or viewing a QR label for compliance assets and clinical kit (fire points, water outlets, fridges, anaphylaxis boxes, emergency drug kits) loaded the QR image from this public third-party API, sending the code's content in the request URL. Primovex now generates QR codes on the device, so labels make no external request at all, and the desktop app's Content-Security-Policy no longer permits the service. Kept here as a record of the earlier flow.

Data shared now: noneData shared previously: an internal asset identifier only (e.g. MEDTRAK:COMPLIANCE:<asset id>), plus, unavoidably, the practice's IP address and the time of each requestNever shared: any patient, staff, case or SAR data

Recommended next step

For each body above marked "to confirm," ask your IG/WASPI contact (likely via BCUHB) whether an existing Information Sharing Protocol already covers "GP practice governance correspondence with [body]," or whether this specific flow needs its own ISP or Data Disclosure Agreement. Several of these (BCUHB itself, the health-board-mediated bodies) are likely already covered by standing NHS Wales arrangements; the ones most likely to need a fresh look are the less routine ones — coroner, police, and solicitor correspondence — since those tend to be handled case-by-case rather than under a standing agreement. The QR image service above is resolved and needs no protocol — just a line in the DPIA noting the earlier flow and its removal.

Added 4 November 2026. For the Microsoft and Google services above, ask for the data processing agreement and the region of each resource to be put on file, which is a contract matter, not a WASPI one. For the shared folder, record which Microsoft account holds it and who it is shared with. Do not use real patient letters in ClinFlow until it has been added to the DPIA and its decisions are made, because that is the point at which the Microsoft services would start to receive patient data.
